# Test 1 — Operator script

Everything the operator says, written out. Read this once end to end before
Day 1. Keep it open on your phone during the pilot.

**Three rules that override everything else in this file.**

0. **You are the list. There is no other list.** Nobody but you ever sees what
   anyone has listed. Do not share the spreadsheet, do not forward a Form
   response, do not broadcast "books available this week" to any group, do not
   pin a sheet of titles to a noticeboard, do not read titles out at the
   stall, and do not turn your laptop or phone screen towards a participant.
   If someone asks "what have you got?", the answer is script §6l. The moment
   students can browse each other's books, BookHop is an e-commerce entity and
   the law requires it to be an incorporated company, which it is not yet.
   This single rule is the difference between a legal pilot and an illegal
   one, and it costs nothing to keep.

1. **Say the bracketed scripts as written.** They are not suggestions. Four of
   the five numbers this pilot produces are only comparable between people if
   every person heard the same words. Fill in `[square brackets]`, change
   nothing else.
2. **Never argue a participant out of going around BookHop.** If someone says
   they will just deal with the other student directly next time, your job is
   to write it down, not to change their mind. That behaviour is the single
   most important thing this pilot measures. Persuading them destroys the
   measurement and you cannot get it back.

**Words you may never say or write to a participant**, in any message, poster,
or conversation:

> safe · safely · secure · verified · vouched · guaranteed · assured ·
> trusted · certified · checked by us · we make sure · don't worry

Say instead: *"the meeting points are in the open, in daylight, where people
are around"* · *"the seller described it as GOOD; look at it yourself"* ·
*"we pass on what the student told us"*.

This is not style. A platform that holds itself out as providing safety or as
vouching for goods takes on liability for both. See `evidence/COMPLIANCE.md`
§2.5 and §5.5.

---

## 1. Seeding a seller — Days 1 to 4, in person

Walk up to final-year and passed-out students in the canteen, outside the
library, in hostel common rooms. They are the ones holding last year's books.

> "Hi — two minutes? I'm running a two-week experiment on campus. No app, no
> company, nothing to download. If you've got last year's prescribed books
> sitting in your room, I'm trying to find the junior who needs that exact
> edition, by hand, off a form. You set the price, or give it away, your call.
> You meet them at the canteen courtyard or the library steps, you hand it
> over, that's it. Want me to send you the form?"

If yes:

> "What's your number? I'll WhatsApp the link now. Takes about ninety
> seconds. The only thing I'd ask is get the edition and the year off the
> title page — that's the whole trick, juniors need the exact edition the
> syllabus names."

If they ask what you get out of it:

> "Nothing this fortnight. I'm finding out whether this is worth building at
> all. If almost nobody uses it, that's the answer and I stop."

**If a club secretary, class rep or hostel warden offers to post the form in a
group** — yes, take it, and write the group name into `source_channel`. That
is the cheapest supply you will get all fortnight.

**Do not** offer money, vouchers, food or any incentive to list. Every rupee
you spend to get registrations goes into the CAC number and the pilot fails
at Rs 15 per registration. Posters and your own voice are the budget.

## 2. Seeding a buyer — Day 4 onwards

Only after Gate A (§1 of the kit) has passed. Target first-years and
second-years at the start of the term.

> "Are you short of any prescribed book this semester? I'm running a two-week
> thing matching people on campus who have the exact prescribed edition with
> people who need it. It's a form and me. No app. Want the link?"

If they say they'll just photocopy it or use the PDF:

> "Fair enough — that's most people. Can I ask which book, and whether it's
> the whole book you need or just a few chapters? That's useful to me either
> way."

Write the answer in `notes`. That is free evidence about the photocopy shop
and it costs you nothing.

## 3. At intake — when a form lands

You are not calling anyone at intake. Log it, and send one WhatsApp:

> "Got your listing — [book title], [edition]. It's in. Nobody else can see
> it; it just sits with me until I find someone who needs that edition, and
> then I'll message you. Meetings are at the canteen courtyard, the library
> steps or the main gate board, between 9 and 6."

**Start the stopwatch when you open the form response. Stop it when the row is
complete in the sheet and that message has been sent.** Put the reading in
`min_intake`.

## 4. The match introduction

Open envelope number N. N is the next unopened envelope. You do not get to
pick, you do not get to look ahead, and you open it after you have decided
who matches whom, never before. Write the envelope number and the arm it
contains into the row, then create a WhatsApp group with both people and
paste one of the two scripts below.

### 4a. FREE arm — paste verbatim

```
Hi both - this is the BookHop pilot. Match code M-[nnn].

[Seller first name] has: [title], [edition/year], described as [CONDITION].
Asking Rs [price].
[Buyer first name] is looking for that book.

To be clear about what this is: I passed on what [seller first name] wrote.
I have not seen the book and BookHop does not check, grade or stand behind
it. [Buyer first name], look at the book yourself before any money moves -
that is your only protection and it works.

Pick one of these three points and a time between 9am and 6pm:
  P1 canteen courtyard - P2 library steps - P3 main gate notice board

Whatever you settle on price is between you two. BookHop takes nothing and
touches no money.

Afterwards, both of you fill this in - thirty seconds, separately:
[Form B link]
```

### 4b. FEE arm — paste verbatim

```
Hi both - this is the BookHop pilot. Match code M-[nnn].

[Seller first name] has: [title], [edition/year], described as [CONDITION].
Asking Rs [price].
[Buyer first name] is looking for that book.

To be clear about what this is: I passed on what [seller first name] wrote.
I have not seen the book and BookHop does not check, grade or stand behind
it. [Buyer first name], look at the book yourself before any money moves -
that is your only protection and it works.

This match is in the fee group. BookHop's fee for a match is 6% of the asking
price [seller first name] listed, which here is Rs [6% of the intake asking
price, rounded to the nearest rupee]. BookHop cannot accept money during this
pilot, so the fee is not paid to us: [buyer first name] hands it to [seller
first name] in cash, separately, on top of whatever you two agree for the
book. Then both of you tick the fee question in the form.

Pick one of these three points and a time between 9am and 6pm:
  P1 canteen courtyard - P2 library steps - P3 main gate notice board

Whatever you settle on price is between you two. BookHop takes nothing and
touches no money.

Afterwards, both of you fill this in - thirty seconds, separately:
[Form B link]
```

**Nothing else may be said about the fee unless someone asks.** Do not soften
it, do not apologise for it, do not explain that it is optional, and do not
chase it afterwards. If asked, use §6a.

## 5. At the handover point, if you are there

Say this once, to both, then step back out of earshot:

> "You two sort it out. One thing: have a proper look at the book first —
> check the edition on the title page, flip through for missing pages —
> before anything else happens. When you're done, both of you fill the form."

Then step away. Do not watch the payment. Do not ask what they settled on. If
you see money change hands, that is fine and you write nothing about the
amount anywhere.

## 6. Refusals, awkwardness and the things that will actually happen

### 6a. "Why should I pay a fee? / What if I don't pay it?"

> "Nothing happens to you. Nobody's chasing it and nobody's penalised. It's
> half the matches in this pilot, at random, and I'm finding out what people
> actually do when they're asked. Either answer is useful to me — I just need
> you both to tick the right box in the form."

Then stop talking about it. Do not negotiate, do not reduce it, do not offer
to waive it. Record what happens.

### 6b. "We'll just handle it ourselves / I'll WhatsApp him next time"

> "Totally fine. Would you still fill the form in afterwards? Even one line
> — just whether the book changed hands. That's the bit I actually need."

**Nothing further.** Do not express disappointment, do not explain why going
through BookHop is better. Log it in `notes`, verbatim, in their words.

### 6c. "Is this safe?"

> "I can't promise you anything about another person, and I'm not going to
> pretend otherwise. What I can tell you is the three meeting points are out
> in the open with people around, meetings are daylight only, and you look at
> the book before any money moves. If you'd rather bring a friend, bring a
> friend. If anything feels off, walk away and message me."

Never answer "yes".

### 6d. "Can you hold the money until I've seen the book?"

> "No — I don't handle money at all, and that's deliberate. You look at the
> book standing in front of them, and if it's not what was described you walk
> away before you've paid anything. That's the whole protection."

### 6e. The age check, and "I'm 17"

**Before you match anybody, ask them out loud, in person or on the phone:**

> "One thing I have to ask everyone — you're 18 or over, yeah?"

Ask it even though they ticked the box. Ask first-years especially; a lot of
first-years are 17 and most of them have not thought about it. Then fill in
`age_18_checked_by` and `age_18_checked_datetime`. No date of birth, no ID,
no proof — we do not collect any of that. You are asking a question and
writing down that you asked it.

If the answer is no, or they hesitate and then say yes:

> "Then I can't match you, sorry — pilot's 18 and over only. Nothing personal
> and nothing you've done wrong. I'm deleting what you entered today, so
> nothing of yours is kept."

Delete the Form A response the same day. Keep the row with
`age_18_declared = NO`, `listing_status = REJECTED_UNDER_18`, **and every
personal field blank** — no name, no roll number, no phone. Count it. Two of
these after a match has been made means the gate is not working and §9 of the
kit applies.

### 6f. "Can I have your personal number?" / someone is persistent after hours

> "The pilot number's the one to use — I check it between 9 and 6."

Do not give out a personal number. Do not meet anyone off campus. Do not
agree to any meeting outside 09:00–18:00.

### 6g. A no-show

To the person who turned up, within the hour:

> "Sorry about that. I'll chase them. Do you want me to try again tomorrow,
> or find you someone else?"

To the person who did not:

> "Did something come up? No problem either way — just tell me yes or no so I
> can close the record."

`handover_outcome = NO_SHOW`. Time spent on this goes in `min_coord`. One
reschedule maximum, then `CANCELLED`.

### 6h. "The book isn't what he said it was"

> "Did you pay anything? ... Right. If you haven't, just don't — that's what
> looking first is for. If you have, I can't get your money back; BookHop
> doesn't hold any money and can't refund. What I can do is record it against
> that listing and stop matching that book."

`dispute_flag = YES`, write what was said in `dispute_note`, delist the book.
Time goes in `min_after`. Never offer compensation. Never pay anyone out of
the pilot budget to settle a dispute.

### 6i. A photocopied or pirated book turns up

> "I can't list that one — pilot's for original copies only. No problem with
> you, it's just a line I can't cross."

Delist, log it, count it. Three of these and §9 of the kit applies.

### 6j. Someone asks what happens to their data

> "Your name, roll number and number are in a spreadsheet on my laptop and in
> a Google Form. If you get matched, that one person gets your first name and
> your number. Everything is deleted on 31 January 2027, or today if you tell
> me to. Nobody else sees it and it isn't sold to anyone."

If they ask to be deleted: do it within one working day, confirm by message,
and keep the row with every personal field blanked and
`listing_status = DELETED_ON_REQUEST`.

### 6k. A crowd forms at your stall, or an argument starts

Stop the conversation, move to the open, do not raise your voice, do not get
between two people. If anyone is pushed, threatened, followed, or frightened
— **stop the pilot now** and call the founder the same hour. See §9 of the
kit. There is no judgement call to make here and it is not yours to make.

### 6l. "So what books have you got? Can I see the list?"

> "There isn't a list you can look at — it's just me and a form. Tell me what
> you need, including the edition if your syllabus names one, and I'll go
> looking. If I find it I'll message you."

Never read out titles, never show a screen, never say "I've got three copies
of Grewal going". Even reading out one title you happen to remember is the
thing rule 0 forbids. Take their requirement, put it in the sheet, and work
it in the matching run.

### 6m. Something goes wrong with the data itself

The laptop or the pilot phone is lost or stolen; the spreadsheet is emailed
or shared to anyone outside the pilot, even by accident; the Google account
is accessed by someone else; a printed sheet with names and numbers goes
missing.

> Tell the founder **within the hour, and in any case inside six hours.**

Do not try to fix it quietly first. There is a six-hour reporting clock that
applies to this pilot today, and it starts when you notice, not when you
finish worrying about it. Write down the time you noticed.

### 6n. A member of staff or the administration asks what you are doing

> "I'm a student running a two-week experiment for a project — matching people
> who have last year's prescribed books with people who need them. No money
> goes through me, nothing's being sold on campus, and I'm happy to stop and
> show you the form."

Then message the founder the same day. Do not sign anything. Do not agree to
anything in writing.

## 7. Chasing a confirmation

At +2 hours after the scheduled handover, if no Form B from either side:

> "How'd it go? Thirty-second form when you get a sec: [link]"

At +24 hours, if still nothing:

> "Just the form when you have a moment — [link]. Even if it didn't happen,
> or if you two sorted it out between yourselves. Either is fine, I just need
> to close the record."

At +72 hours, if still nothing, **message each person separately — never in
the group**, and ask only this. Word for word:

To the seller:

> "Last thing from me, one question: do you still have the [title]? Yes or
> no is enough."

To the buyer:

> "Last thing from me, one question: did you end up getting the [title]? Yes
> or no is enough."

That is the whole follow-up. Do not ask whether they paid. Do not ask how
much. Do not ask why they did not fill the form. Do not ask whether they went
around you. One question each, and then you stop.

Three attempts total. After the third, mark `followup_reply_* = NO_REPLY` and
leave it. Do not call. Do not visit their hostel. Do not ask their friends.
All time spent chasing goes in `min_chase`.

## 8. Daily close — say nothing, write everything

At 18:00 every day, before you go home:

1. Every stopwatch reading entered. No reading left in your head or on paper.
2. Every rupee spent today entered as a `SPEND` row, with the receipt
   photographed.
3. Unallocated time (standing at the stall, putting up posters, walking)
   entered as a `DAILY_UNALLOCATED` row. This is **not** support time and must
   never go into a `min_*` column.
4. Running tally into the day's line of the kit's §10 tracker: registrations,
   live listings, matches, completed handovers, spend to date.

If you have not logged for two consecutive days, stop the pilot and tell the
founder. Reconstructed timings are not data and the whole test rests on them.
