## 0. Read this first

You are about to run a two-week experiment at one Chennai campus. There is no
app and no code. The whole thing is **a Google Form, a spreadsheet, one person
and a stopwatch**. You do not need to have read any other document in this
repository. Everything you need is in this file and the three files beside it:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `test1/forms.md` | The two forms, word for word, to paste into Google Forms |
| `test1/operator-script.md` | Everything you say out loud, word for word |
| `test1/tracking-sheet.csv` | The spreadsheet, with its headers and two example rows |

**What this pilot is for.** Three numbers decide whether BookHop is a business,
and none of them has ever been observed: what a completed handover costs in
human attention, what a registration costs to acquire, and how many
transactions a student does. A fourth question is open and is the sharpest
objection anyone has raised against the product: **the moment a buyer inspects
a book before paying is the same moment the pair can decide never to tell the
platform.** Every design in this file exists to measure one of those four
things, and anything that did not measure one of them has been cut.

**What this pilot is not for.** It is not a launch, not a growth exercise, and
not a demo. Nobody is being sold anything. If the honest answer is that almost
nobody uses it, that answer is worth the whole Rs 16,500 and you should be as
pleased to find it as the opposite.

**Two principles order everything below.**

**Liquidity before demand.** A buyer whose first search returns nothing never
comes back, and a pilot that opens demand before it has supply measures
nothing except its own impatience. So the first three days have no buyers in
them at all.

**The operator is the list, and there is no other list.** No participant ever
sees what anyone else has offered — no shared sheet, no group broadcast of
available books, no printed list on a noticeboard, no screen turned around.
Every match runs through the human. This is not a simplification, it is the
line that keeps the pilot legal: a surface students can browse makes BookHop
an e-commerce entity, and the rules then require an incorporated company that
does not yet exist. Keeping the rule costs nothing. Breaking it stops the
pilot (§9, K11).

**You are not blocked waiting on the company.** Test 1 needs no incorporation
and no legal spend. Incorporation is the founder's, costs Rs 8,000–20,000,
sits outside this Rs 16,500 envelope, and runs in parallel.

**Dates.** The pilot runs 14 consecutive days at a semester boundary, Day 1 to
Day 14. Day 0 is preparation and is not one of the fourteen. Write the real
dates into the header of the tracking sheet on Day 0 and do not move them.

## 1. The operating protocol

### 1.1 Day 0 — preparation (half a day, before the clock starts)

Nothing on this list is optional and none of it can be done later.

1. **Campus permission in hand.** The founder has it in writing, or the pilot
   does not start. Agents cannot obtain it (founder-reserved authority).
2. **Name the three handover points.** They must be outdoors or in an open
   public indoor space, on campus, overlooked by other people, and busy
   between 09:00 and 18:00. The defaults used throughout these documents are
   P1 canteen courtyard, P2 library steps, P3 main gate notice board. Replace
   those names with the campus's real ones everywhere, including in the forms.
3. **Both forms live** in Google Forms, pasted from `test1/forms.md`, each
   writing to its own response sheet. Submit one test response to each and
   check it lands. Delete the test responses. **Turn OFF "See summary charts
   and text responses"** — no participant may ever see another's answers.
4. **Tracking sheet open**, from `test1/tracking-sheet.csv`, with the two
   EXAMPLE rows deleted. **Version history on and never cleared**, sharing
   set to the operator and the founder only, and nobody else added for any
   reason during the fourteen days.
5. **Randomisation envelopes sealed** — see §2.1. The **founder** does this,
   not the operator.
6. **Posters and handbills printed** — see §10.
7. **A separate pilot phone number** (a cheap prepaid SIM, or a second
   WhatsApp on a spare handset). Never the operator's personal number.
8. **Stopwatch.** The phone's own stopwatch is fine. Practise starting and
   stopping it until it is automatic, because you will do it forty times a day.
9. **Scoring sheet signed — the pre-registration lock.** Print §7, fill in
   every threshold, and have the founder and the operator both sign and date
   it. See §7.0 for the lock itself.
10. **Operator briefed**: reads `operator-script.md` end to end, and does one
    dry run of intake-to-match against a fake listing.

### 1.2 The standard day

Every day from Day 1 to Day 13 runs on this clock. What changes day to day is
the objective, in §1.3, not the clock.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 08:30–09:00 | **Open** | Read overnight form responses. Log each into the sheet. Stopwatch on each (`min_intake`). Send the one-line acknowledgement from script §3. |
| 09:00–10:30 | **Field block A** | Days 1–4: supply seeding on foot (script §1). Days 5–13: coordination — reschedules, chases, anything stuck. |
| 10:30–11:00 | **Matching run 1** | One stopwatch run across the whole batch. Pair requests to live listings. Open envelopes, send introductions (script §4). Record the batch total and the number of matches produced. |
| 11:00–13:00 | **Stall** | Table at P1 with the poster, the QR to Form A, and a pen. **No books on the table and no list of titles anywhere on it** — the stall advertises the pilot, never the stock. This is where most registrations come from. Standing time is logged as `DAILY_UNALLOCATED`, never as support minutes. |
| 13:00–14:00 | **Handover window A** | The lunch peak. Be at the point if any handover is scheduled. Say script §5 once, then step away. |
| 14:00–16:00 | **Field block B** | Department noticeboards, hostel wings, club and class-rep contacts. Days 1–4 this is the main work of the day. |
| 16:00–17:00 | **Matching run 2** | As the morning run. |
| 17:00–18:00 | **Handover window B** | The end-of-class peak. |
| 18:00–18:45 | **Close** | Script §8: all stopwatch readings entered, all spend entered with receipts photographed, unallocated time entered, the day's tally written into §1.4. |

Two staffed windows exist so that handovers cluster where you can observe
them. They are **not** compulsory: a pair may meet at any of the three points
at any time between 09:00 and 18:00, and `handover_staffed` records which it
was. Forcing every handover into a staffed window would make the pilot
unrepresentative of a product where nobody is watching.

unknown band in §4. It is never guessed at.

### 1.5 Gate A — the liquidity gate, 18:00 on Day 3

| Live listings at 18:00 Day 3 | Action |
|---|---|
| **40 or more**, across 10+ distinct prescribed titles | Open demand on Day 4 as planned. |
| **25 to 39** | Open demand on Day 4, and spend field block B of Days 4–6 on supply, not demand. |
| **15 to 24** | Do not open demand. Spend Day 4 entirely on supply. Re-gate at 18:00 Day 4. |
| **Under 15 at 18:00 on Day 4** | **Stop the pilot.** See §9. Students will not list, which is a complete answer, and no amount of demand spending changes it. Report it and keep the remaining money. |

### 1.6 Mid-point review — 18:00 on Day 7

Not a gate, a forecast. Compute `completed handovers to date ÷ 7 × 14`. If the
projection is under 20, you will not reach 30 and you should say so to the
founder that evening rather than on Day 14. The pilot continues either way —
a measured shortfall is a result — but the founder gets a week of notice.

Also check acquisition spend against §10. If it is above the pre-authorised
Rs 1,250, stop spending. `marketplace-ops` may halt supply-side spend under
its charter; it may not authorise more.

## 2. The 6%-versus-free A/B

### 2.1 Assignment: how the operator is prevented from steering

The risk this design defends against is not dishonesty. It is an operator who
likes a particular student, senses the fee will annoy them, and puts them in
the free arm without ever deciding to. Everything below exists to remove the
opportunity.

**The randomisation is made by the founder, on Day 0, before a single
participant exists.**

1. Generate a sequence of 60 assignments **in blocks of four**: within each
   block of four, two FEE and two FREE, shuffled. A coin works (HH→reshuffle;
   simplest is to write FEE FEE FREE FREE on four slips per block and draw
   them). Blocks of four guarantee the two arms stay near-balanced no matter
   how many matches the pilot actually produces — which matters, because
   nobody knows whether that number will be 12 or 50.
2. Write each assignment on a slip, seal it in an **opaque** envelope, and
   number the envelopes 1 to 60 on the outside. Tape the stack in order.
3. The founder keeps a **sealed duplicate** of the sequence and does not open
   it until Day 14. If the sheet and the duplicate disagree, the pilot's A/B
   result is void and is reported as void.
4. The operator never sees the sequence and cannot see ahead.

**At the moment of a match, in this order and no other:**

1. Decide who matches whom. This decision is made on the book, never on the
   person, and it is made **before** any envelope is touched.
2. Open the next unopened envelope. Not a different one.
3. Write the envelope number and the arm it contains into the row
   (`arm_envelope_no`, `arm`) **before** sending the introduction.
4. Paste the matching script from `operator-script.md` §4a or §4b.

**Four further locks.**

- **Numbers in order.** Every envelope number is in the sheet. A skipped or
  out-of-order number is visible at scoring, by anyone, in five seconds.
- **Intention-to-treat.** A match enters the denominator the instant the
  introduction message is sent, and it never leaves. No match may be removed,
  voided or re-rolled afterwards — not for a no-show, not for a block, not
  because the pair turned out to know each other. This closes the other
  steering route: quietly dropping fee-arm matches that look doomed.
- **Sticky by buyer.** If a buyer appears in a second match, they keep the arm
  from their first match and **no new envelope is opened**; record
  `arm_envelope_no = CARRIED-<first number>`. This prevents the same person
  being asked for a fee on Tuesday and not on Thursday, which would teach them
  the fee is negotiable and contaminate both arms.
- **The ask is pasted, not spoken.** The fee exists in a fixed block of text.
  An operator cannot under-deliver it with their tone of voice if they do not
  deliver it with their voice at all.

### 2.2 What each arm is told, verbatim

Both scripts are in `operator-script.md` §4a (free) and §4b (fee) and must be
pasted unaltered. They are identical except for one paragraph. The fee
paragraph reads:

> This match is in the fee group. BookHop's fee for a match is 6% of the
> asking price [seller first name] listed, which here is Rs [6% of the intake
> asking price, rounded to the nearest rupee]. BookHop cannot accept money
> during this pilot, so the fee is not paid to us: [buyer first name] hands
> it to [seller first name] in cash, separately, on top of whatever you two
> agree for the book. Then both of you tick the fee question in the form.

Three things about that paragraph are deliberate.

- **6% of the asking price as fixed at intake, not of the settled price, and
  not of a price revised later.** It is computed once, from
  `asking_price_at_intake_inr`, written into `fee_due_inr` at the moment of
  matching, and never recalculated. We never learn the settled price and must
  not record one; a fee that tracked a renegotiated price would be a fee on a
  consideration, which is the thing we may not have. See §8.
- **The money moves student to student and never through BookHop.** This is
  not squeamishness. The moment BookHop collects or settles money there is a
  determinable gross amount, s194-O/s393 bites, and PAN or Aadhaar collection
  becomes unavoidable — a data liability out of all proportion to 6% of a
  Rs 220 book (`evidence/COMPLIANCE.md` §4.3, §4.4, blocker P14, ledger L2:
  tax counsel signs off before any fee). BookHop receives nothing in this
  pilot and holds nothing at any point.
- **Nothing is said about what happens if they decline.** Not because we are
  hiding it — if asked, script §6a answers truthfully that nothing happens —
  but because volunteering it converts the ask into an invitation to refuse.

### 2.3 Recording fee-asked and fee-paid without operator judgement

| Quantity | Where it comes from | Operator's part in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Fee asked** | The envelope. `arm = FEE` is a fact of randomisation recorded before the introduction is sent. | None. It is not an operator report. |
| **Fee ask delivered** | Form B Q7, answered independently by each participant: *"Were you told that a 6% pilot fee applies to this match?"* | None. Cross-checks the operator against the envelope; disagreements are reported as a protocol-fidelity number, not quietly fixed. |
| **Fee paid** | Form B Q8, answered **separately by the buyer and by the seller**: *"Did that fee get paid, in cash?"* `fee_paid_both_agree = YES` only when both independently answered yes. | None. The operator never sees the money, never asks about it, and never chases it. |

If only one side says yes, `fee_paid_both_agree = NO`. It is not a half.
There is no partial credit and no operator discretion anywhere in this
measurement.

**What the cash-only rule cost this measurement, stated rather than hidden.**
The design above originally had the two parties independently report the last
four digits of a UPI reference, which two strangers cannot match by accident
and which no amount of politeness can fake. The compliance envelope for this
pilot permits no payment identifier of any kind, so the fee is cash and that
cross-check is gone. **Fee-paid now rests on two independent self-reports with
nothing behind them.** Two people who want to look cooperative can both tick
yes. Nobody has an incentive to falsely tick *no*. The asymmetry that survives
is therefore the same one §2.4 describes, and it is the only thing this number
is good for: **a low fee-collection figure is credible; a high one is not.**

### 2.4 The honest limitation — read this before believing the number

**A true platform fee cannot be measured in this pilot, and the number this
design produces is a proxy with a known direction of bias.**

A real 6% fee takes money out of the pair and gives it to the platform. Both
parties are then better off settling privately, and that joint incentive is
precisely what objection O-10 is about. In this pilot BookHop may not receive
money (§2.2), and money that stays inside the pair is not a fee — it is a
transfer. Whichever direction it moves, somebody in the conversation gains
from it and argues for it:

| Construction | Who loses | Who argues for it | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer pays 6% extra to the seller (**chosen**) | buyer | seller | **Overstates** collection: the seller is standing there, advocating. |
| Seller gives up 6% to the buyer | seller | buyer | Mirror image, same flaw. |
| Fee goes to a third party outside the pair | both | nobody | Reproduces the real incentive — but requires a recipient, and the only honest one available is a charity, which is a donation, not a fee. Rejected: it measures generosity. |

Against that upward bias runs one downward bias: the fee is unenforceable and
the operator never chases it, which a real product fee would not be. And
sitting across both is the self-report problem from §2.3 — the fee is cash,
so there is no artefact anywhere that corroborates either answer.

The biases run in opposite directions, nobody can say in what proportion, and
the underlying observation is unverifiable. Therefore the rule, decided now:

> **A low fee-collection number is strong evidence. A high one is weak
> evidence.** If collection comes in under 40% even with the seller arguing in
> its favour, the transaction fee is dead and should never be designed around
> again. If it comes in over 40%, that does **not** reinstate the fee: it
> reopens the question for a second, properly-powered test in which BookHop
> actually receives the money, which requires tax counsel first (`COMPLIANCE`
> ledger L2).

This is a deliberate deviation from the literal wording of the reversal
trigger in D-006 and D-007, flagged here for `ceo-planning` to accept or
reject. **The half of the A/B that is clean is the other half** — the
completion-rate difference between the arms — because both arms experience
the same everything except the existence of the ask. That is the number to
weight, and §7 scores it accordingly.

## 3. Instrumentation

### 3.1 The stopwatch rule

> **Start the watch when you begin doing something to one specific record.
> Stop it when you stop. If you switch records, stop and start again.**

Readings are in minutes to one decimal place (0.1 min = 6 seconds). Anything
shorter than six seconds is recorded as 0.1, never as 0. Nothing is ever
reconstructed from memory: if you did not time it, the entry is `UNTIMED` and
that record is excluded from the support-time calculation and counted in the
exclusion line of §7.

### 3.2 The six buckets

| Column | Starts when | Stops when | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `min_intake` | You open a new Form A response | The row is complete and the acknowledgement is sent | Deciphering a handwritten title, looking up an ISBN, rejecting an under-18 |
| `min_match` | You begin a matching run | The last introduction of that run is sent | See the batch rule below |
| `min_coord` | You open a message about an already-introduced match | You close it | Reschedules, nudges, questions, no-show handling, a pair who go quiet |
| `min_handover` | You start speaking to, or about, this specific pair at the point | You step away | **Not** standing time — see §3.3 |
| `min_after` | Any contact about this match after the handover | It ends | Disputes, complaints, a returned book, a question about the fee |
| `min_chase` | You start the +2h, +24h or +72h follow-up for this match | You send it | Each attempt adds to the same cell |

`min_total` is the sum of the six. In the spreadsheet:
`=SUM(min_intake:min_chase)`.

**The batch rule for matching.** Matching is done in runs, not one at a time.
Time the whole run with one stopwatch reading, count the matches the run
produced, and divide equally: `min_match = run total ÷ matches produced`, to
one decimal. Record the run total and the count in `notes` so the division is
reproducible by anyone. A run that produces no match has its minutes recorded
as `DAILY_UNALLOCATED` — real cost, but not attributable to any transfer.

### 3.3 What is not support time

Standing at the stall, walking between buildings, putting up posters,
briefing a class rep, waiting at a handover point before the pair arrives, and
the daily close itself. All of it goes into a single `DAILY_UNALLOCATED` row
per day, with the total minutes in `min_total` and nothing in the other five
columns.

This is excluded on purpose: in a product, nobody is standing at the canteen.
Including it would measure the pilot's scaffolding rather than the
transaction. It is reported separately in §7 because it is a real cost of
running a campus, and ignoring it entirely would be the opposite error.

### 3.4 The two support numbers, and which one matters

**Support minutes per completed handover (median)** — the pass/fail number.
For each **completed** handover, take `min_total` of its listing row. Take the
median across completed handovers. Threshold: **≤ 4.00 minutes**.

**Fully-loaded support minutes per completed handover (mean)** — the number
that goes into the financial model. Sum `min_total` across **every** record in
the sheet, including matches that failed, no-shows, disputes, rejected
listings and listings that never matched. Divide by the number of completed
handovers. Nothing is excluded for being a failure; failures are what support
cost is made of.

**Chase minutes, reported separately and never excluded from the pass
number.** `min_chase` is the +2h, +24h and +72h follow-up. It exists because
of §4 — the pilot chases records that a product would not chase, or would
chase automatically — so it is partly the instrument's own cost rather than
the transaction's. Report the fully-loaded mean twice: **including
`min_chase`, which is the pre-registered pass number**, and excluding it,
labelled *"instrument cost removed, not the pass number"*. The second figure
is there so the founder can see how much of the operating cost is the
measuring and how much is the business. It is never used to turn a fail into
a pass, and if the two figures straddle the threshold the metric is a fail,
not a debate.

Convert to rupees at **Rs 2.84 per minute** (Rs 30,000/month fully loaded,
from `evidence/RED-TEAM/G1-MEMO.md` O-4):

```
support cost per transfer (Rs) = fully-loaded mean minutes x 2.84
```

**Read the result against two lines, not one.**

| Minutes | Rupees | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4.00 | Rs 11.36 | The pass threshold. Roughly confirms the Rs 12 the model already assumes. |
| **2.11** | **Rs 6.00** | The number at which a blend of individually-defensible drivers turns the unit positive (G1-MEMO O-4: +Rs 2.71/transfer). |
| 1.06 | Rs 3.00 | The number at which support alone flips the sign (G1-MEMO O-3). |

**Passing the 4-minute bar means "not catastrophic", not "viable".** Say that
sentence in the write-up. A pilot that lands at 3.9 minutes has confirmed the
pessimistic assumption, not refuted it, and anyone reading only the
pass/fail column will get that backwards.

### 3.5 The other defined measurements

| Field | Definition — exact enough that two operators record the same thing |
|---|---|
| **Registration** | One distinct person who submitted Form A with a roll number and declared on it that they are 18 or over. Counted once, by `person_ref`, however many books they list or request. Submissions declaring under 18, and anyone who says no to the operator's in-person check, are **not** registrations and are deleted the same day. |
| **Live listing** | A `LISTING` row with `listing_status = LIVE` or `MATCHED`. Delisted, withdrawn, rejected and sold rows are not live. |
| **Match introduced** | The timestamp the group introduction message was sent. Nothing before that counts, and once counted it is permanent. |
| **Completed handover** | **Both** parties independently state that the book changed hands — in Form B, or at the 72-hour follow-up, or one of each. One side alone is never a completion. |
| **No-show** | A scheduled handover where one party attended at the agreed point within 15 minutes of the agreed time and the other did not. |
| **Acquisition spend** | Cash from these four lines only: poster printing, handbill printing, stall signage, any participant incentive. Fixed on Day 0; see §10. Operator stipend, travel, SIM and stationery are **excluded** — they are operating cost, not acquisition. |
| **CAC** | Acquisition spend ÷ registrations. Cash only. **Excludes the operator's and founder's labour**, which at scale would dominate it. State that caveat every time the number is quoted. |
| **Transactions per student** | Completed handovers ÷ distinct people appearing in at least one completed handover. Over 14 days, not a year. Report it as a fortnight figure and **never annualise it** — the whole point of R-04 is that the year is not uniform. |

## 4. Silent-delisting detection

### 4.0 What this measurement is for — it changed, and it got more important

When the fee existed, a pair settling privately was a **revenue** leak: the 6%
walked away. v1 has no fee (D-006, D-007), so nothing walks away. What is lost
instead is **the completion record** — the evidence that a specific student
got a specific prescribed book on a specific day.

Under the revised thesis the hypothesised payer is the institution
(`README.md` §1), and an institution is not buying matches. It is buying a
record of provision it can put against a budget line. If pairs meet through
BookHop and then transact in silence, BookHop still works and BookHop still
has nothing to sell.

So this measurement is not a side-count any more. It is the one that tests
whether the surviving business model has an asset. Two consequences, both
applied below:

1. **It earns its cost.** The 72-hour follow-up stays, in full, on every
   unresolved match.
2. **But its cost lands on another threshold, and that must be visible.**
   Chasing records consumes operator minutes, those minutes are support
   minutes, and support minutes are Threshold 2. The pilot's instrument
   inflates the pilot's own result. §3.4 handles this by reporting the
   support figure twice — once including chase minutes, which is the
   pre-registered pass number, and once with them separated out and labelled
   as instrument cost. The exclusion is never used to turn a fail into a pass.

### 4.1 What can be measured cleanly, and how

A pair who meet through the pilot and settle without telling us leave one
trace that is not surveillance: **the book is no longer where it was.** That
is a question about a physical object, it is cheap to answer honestly, and it
says nothing about money.

At 72 hours after a scheduled handover, where no Form B arrived, the operator
sends **one question to each party, separately, never in the group**
(`operator-script.md` §7):

- to the seller: *"do you still have the [title]?"*
- to the buyer: *"did you end up getting the [title]?"*

Two independent answers about one object. Compare them and classify:

| `leakage_class` | Seller says | Buyer says | Form B | Completed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `IN_APP_CONFIRMED` | — | — | both filed, book moved | **Yes** |
| `ONE_SIDED_CONFIRMED` | moved | moved | one filed | **Yes** |
| `SILENT_CONFIRMED` | gone | received | neither filed | **Yes** — and fully unreported |
| `SOLD_ELSEWHERE` | gone | did not get it | — | No — leaked outside the match |
| `NOT_MOVED` | still have it | did not get it | — | No |
| `SILENT_PARTIAL` | one says moved | other silent | — | No — unknown band |
| `UNKNOWN_NO_REPLY` | silent | silent | — | No — unknown band |
| `UNKNOWN_TRUNCATED` | window cut by Day 14 | — | — | No — unknown band |

Also set `delisted_within_72h = YES` where a seller withdraws or marks a
listing gone within 72 hours of a match without a Form B. That is O-7's
original observable and it is free to record.

**The silent-delisting rate is reported as a band, never as a point.**

```
known moved      K = IN_APP_CONFIRMED + ONE_SIDED_CONFIRMED + SILENT_CONFIRMED
unknown          U = SILENT_PARTIAL + UNKNOWN_NO_REPLY + UNKNOWN_TRUNCATED

lower bound  =  SILENT_CONFIRMED / K
upper bound  =  (SILENT_CONFIRMED + U) / (K + U)
```

Report both, with K and U printed next to them. If the band is wider than
20 percentage points, the honest statement is that the pilot did not measure
this, and the write-up says so rather than quoting the midpoint. There is no
midpoint.

**Why this is not surveillance.** One question, asked directly, answered
voluntarily, about an object the person owns, under a consent they gave at
intake, with three attempts and then silence. No tracking, no asking their
friends, no inferring from anything they did not tell us, and no question
about money anywhere in it.

### 4.2 What cannot be measured, stated plainly

Three kinds of leakage are invisible to this pilot. None of them has an honest
proxy available for Rs 16,500, and no proxy is offered for them.

1. **A pair who both say the book did not move when it did.** Undetectable,
   by construction. The measurement's lower bound is a floor on leakage, not
   an estimate of it.
2. **A pair who meet through the pilot and transact a *different* book later.**
   Out of scope of any record we hold.
3. **The real O-10 question — do they come back?** Disintermediation is a
   behaviour over months. The observable is repeat transfer at 90 days, and a
   14-day pilot structurally cannot see it. **This pilot cannot measure
   whether a matched pair defects permanently.** It can only measure whether
   they defect *on the first transaction*, which is the easier case for
   BookHop and therefore an optimistic reading.

Form B Q13 asks whether they would come back through BookHop next time. That
is a stated intention about a hypothetical, which is exactly the instrument
objection O-7 struck down. **It is recorded as colour and must never be used
as a threshold, quoted as a percentage, or put in a summary.** The honest
answer to "will they come back" is that we will not know until something runs
for 90 days, and that should be said in the write-up in those words.

### 7.0 The pre-registration lock

Concretely, the following are frozen at the lock: the five thresholds (30,
4.00, 15.00, 40.00, 60.00 and 10.00), the §7.0.1 unknown-handling rule, every
denominator in §7.1 to §7.5, the minimum-n rules, the §7.6 verdict matrix,

```
Operator ______________________  Date ____________
Founder  ______________________  Date ____________

```

That signature and that timestamp are the whole defence against the failure
mode in R-21 — a pilot whose thresholds drift once the data starts looking

than killing them, and a moving threshold is exactly how that happens.

### 7.0.1 Unresolved records — the rule decided before the data arrives

Some records will be unresolved on Day 14. The temptation on Day 14 is to
decide what they probably were. Instead:

> **Compute every metric twice — once treating every `UNKNOWN_*` record as a
> failure, once treating every one as a success. If both computations give the
> same verdict, that is the verdict. If they differ, the metric is
> `INCONCLUSIVE`, which is not a pass.**

Other rules, all decided now:

- Two decimal places throughout. No rounding up to reach a threshold: 3.995
  does not become 4.00 on the wrong side, and 15.01 fails a Rs 15 bar.
- "At least 30" means 30 passes and 29 fails.
- Median with an even count: the mean of the two middle values.
- The pilot is never extended, and no threshold moves. A shortfall is a result.

### 7.1 Threshold 1 — completed handovers ≥ 30

```
A = count of rows where handover_outcome indicates completion
    (leakage_class in IN_APP_CONFIRMED, ONE_SIDED_CONFIRMED, SILENT_CONFIRMED)

PASS if A >= 30
```

Run it twice per §7.0.1, with `SILENT_PARTIAL` and the two `UNKNOWN_*` classes
counted first as not-completed, then as completed.

### 7.2 Threshold 2 — median support minutes per completed handover ≤ 4.00

```
B = list of min_total for the A completed handovers, sorted
    median(B)              -> the pass/fail number        PASS if <= 4.00

C = SUM(min_total) over EVERY row in the sheet            (all records)
    fully-loaded mean = C / A
    support cost per transfer = fully-loaded mean x 2.84  (rupees)
```

Report `median(B)`, the fully-loaded mean, the rupee figure, the
fully-loaded mean **excluding `min_chase`** labelled *"instrument cost
removed, not the pass number"*, the count of `UNTIMED` exclusions, and the
total `DAILY_UNALLOCATED` minutes — six numbers, every time, in that order.
Read the rupee figure against the three lines in §3.4 and write the sentence
from §3.4 into the result.

### 7.3 Threshold 3 — CAC ≤ Rs 15.00

```
D = SUM(spend_amount_inr) over SPEND rows whose spend_purpose = ACQUISITION
E = count of distinct person_ref with age_18_declared = YES

CAC = D / E                                               PASS if <= 15.00
```

Quote it as *"Rs X per registration, cash only, excluding operator and founder
labour"*. Never without that clause.

### 7.4 Threshold 4 — fee collection ≥ 40%

```
F = FEE-arm matches that reached a completed handover      (denominator)
G = those where fee_paid_both_agree = YES                  (numerator)

fee collection = G / F x 100                               PASS if >= 40.00

also report, and label clearly as secondary:
    G / (all FEE-arm matches introduced) x 100
    count where fee_told_buyer or fee_told_seller disagrees with arm  (fidelity)
```

The primary denominator is completed handovers, because a fee cannot be paid
at a handover that never happened and folding no-shows in would measure
no-shows. **Apply §2.3 and §2.4 when reading this number: under 40% is
decisive, over 40% is two self-reports with nothing behind them.**

### 7.5 Threshold 5 — the free arm clears 60%, and the fee arm is within 10 points of it

**Two numbers that the original specification ran together.** A pair can fail
to appear in our records two ways, and they mean opposite things:

- the handover **did not happen** — a no-show, a pair who never met;
- the handover **happened and nobody told us** — the O-10 event.

Threshold 5 is about the second. So it is scored on *reported* completions, and
on a denominator of handovers we know took place:

```
per arm:
  J = count of leakage_class = IN_APP_CONFIRMED          (both sides filed Form B)
  K = known moved  = IN_APP_CONFIRMED + ONE_SIDED_CONFIRMED + SILENT_CONFIRMED

  in-app report rate = J / K x 100
```

```
5a  FREE arm in-app report rate >= 60.00                 absolute floor
5b  (free rate - fee rate) <= 10.00                      relative, fee arm may be higher

PASS requires BOTH.
INCONCLUSIVE if either arm has K < 10.
If 5a fails, 5b is not scored: it is reported as INCONCLUSIVE whatever its value.
```

**Why an absolute floor was added, and why 60%.** The relative test alone can
pass for the wrong reason. If the free arm reports 25% and the fee arm 20%,
the difference is 5 points and the threshold is met — while the true finding
is that almost nobody tells us anything, in either arm, and the fee had
nothing to do with it. That is the identical defect Red Team found in T3: a
threshold that cannot fail. Raised by the Product agent; accepted, because it
is right.

**The free arm is not a second treatment. It is the control for friction.**
With no money at stake, a free-arm pair who do not tell us are not defecting —
they are busy, the form is a nuisance, the operator was slow, or they simply
forgot. That rate is the measurement channel's own noise floor. Only once it
is known can any gap be attributed to the fee.

60% is a bar on the instrument, not on the students. This pilot hands each
pair a card with a link, chases at +2 hours and again at +24, and has a human
whose entire job is to close records. A free handover reported in-app less
than three times in five, under those conditions, means we did not build a
measurement, and no number computed on top of it means anything. The bar is
set at 60 rather than higher because with roughly 15 known-moved handovers per
arm each single record is worth about 7 points, and a higher bar would turn
ordinary sampling noise into a false "inconclusive".

**The product bar would have to be higher than 60%, not lower.** In a product
there is no human chasing anybody. Do not carry 60% forward into the PRD as a
target; it is the floor at which *this pilot* is able to speak.

**Both parts are underpowered and the write-up must say so.** With roughly 15
known-moved handovers per arm, the 95% confidence interval on each arm's rate
is on the order of ±25 percentage points. A 10-point difference cannot be
resolved by this sample. What the test can detect is a large effect — an arm
collapsing — and that is what it is for. A gap inside 10 points is evidence of
"no catastrophe", not evidence of "no effect".

**Reported separately, and not thresholded:** each arm's *true* completion
rate, `(all completions) / (matches introduced)`. That is the no-show and
match-quality number. It is operationally important, it belongs in the
write-up, and it is not what Threshold 5 is about.

**This pilot is not blocked on the company.** Gate A, and the whole of Test 1,
needs neither incorporation nor any legal spend — provided the no-browsable-
surface rule in §8 holds, which is what keeps the pilot outside r.4(1)(a).
Incorporation has weeks of lead time and should run in parallel, on the
founder's clock, not in front of this one.

## 11. What this pilot still will not tell you

Written here so that the result is read correctly, not expansively.

1. **Whether a pair defects permanently.** Fourteen days cannot see a 90-day
   repeat rate (§4.2).
2. **What a real platform fee collects.** Only what a student-to-student
   surcharge collects, with the seller arguing in its favour (§2.4).
3. **Transactions per student per year.** It measures a fortnight at a
   semester boundary, which is the best fortnight of the year. Annualising it
   would be the exact error R-04 warns against.
4. **Whether a second campus behaves like the first.** One campus, one
   operator, one semester boundary.
5. **Search-success rate — R-01's own test.** There is no search in this
   pilot and there cannot be: a browsable surface is what the
   no-e-commerce-entity constraint forbids (§0, §8). What the pilot measures
   instead is **match rate under a human matcher**, which is an upper bound
   on what a search would achieve, because a person can satisfy "the 2022
   edition or near enough" and a string match cannot. R-01 stays open after
   this pilot and needs the product to close it.
5. **Whether the photocopy shop or the free PDF wins.** Those are Test 2's
   question. The operator writes down every "I'd just photocopy it" in
   `notes`, and that free text is handed to Product as colour, not as a
   measurement.
