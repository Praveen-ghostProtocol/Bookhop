## The one-paragraph situation

Phase A killed the **revenue model**, not the product. A 6% fee on a ~Rs 220
local handover cannot cover its own support and payment cost — the minimum
take rate to break even on variable cost alone is 16.7%, and Red Team tried
and failed to break that finding. But Red Team also caught Finance
overreaching: "no breakeven at any volume" is an artifact of a linear model
that contains no mechanism for the unit to improve with density. The company
is not disproven. The *way we planned to make money from students* is.

The sharpest new finding is one nobody had written down: **inspect-before-pay
is the disintermediation event.** Compliance requires it, because we hold no
money and therefore cannot refund. Finance identifies in-person settlement as
the revenue hole. They are the same thirty seconds. That is why the
recommendation below is to stop charging students rather than to charge them
differently.

## Decision 3 — Scope: direct swap and general reading in v1

**This conflicts with your written brief, which is why it is yours and not
mine.** You specified buy, sell **and** exchange, covering academic textbooks
**and** general reading, from the first campus release.

Red Team recommends cutting direct swap and general reading from v1. The
evidence: supply and demand peak in **antiphase** — sellers release books at
semester end, buyers need them at semester start — and direct swap is the
worst-fitted mechanism in an antiphase market, because it requires two people
with mutually matching needs *at the same moment*. It also generates zero
cash. General reading dilutes the only defensible wedge (edition-locked
prescribed textbooks) across a category where we have no advantage at all.

**Options:**
- **(a) Cut both from v1, keep them in the data model.** Red Team's
  recommendation. Fastest path to a testable wedge.
- **(b) Keep your original scope.** Defensible — a swap-only user may be the
  cheapest supply you ever acquire, and that is untested.
- **(c) Keep swap, cut general reading.** Splits the difference.

**Recommendation: (a)**, with swap revisited after Test 1. But I am flagging
rather than deciding, because you set this scope deliberately and the evidence
against swap is about *fit*, not about demand — nobody has asked a student.

## Decision 4 — Monetisation direction

The student-side fee is falsified. Red Team's revised thesis: a **syllabus-to-
shelf matching layer for edition-locked prescribed textbooks on one campus**,
**free to students permanently by design** — no fee, no escrow, no custody.
That single move deletes fee collection, disintermediation-as-revenue-risk,
the entire PPI question and most e-commerce exposure. The hypothesised payer
is the **institution** (library, department, placement cell).

**This is a hypothesis with a Rs 0 / five-day test attached, not a strategy.**
Finance's similar institutional/buyback recommendation was *rejected* for
having zero sources and being contradicted by its own file.

**Needs you because:** it changes what the company is, and the institutional
channel implies a sales motion you would personally own.
**Recommendation:** authorise the Rs 0 test (four questions to a librarian,
folded into the Decision 2 campus visit). Do not commit to the direction.
