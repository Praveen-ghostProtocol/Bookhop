**Owner:** cto · **Version:** v1.0 · **Date:** 2026-10-21 · **Status:** FROZEN
**Frozen means:** a developer agent implements this as written. A change
requires a cto-approved amendment recorded at the bottom of this file, and a

## 0. How to read this

- `id` is `bigint GENERATED BY DEFAULT AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY` unless stated.
- All timestamps are `timestamptz`, `USE_TZ=True`, stored UTC, rendered in
  `Asia/Kolkata`. **Every SLA clock in §7 depends on this; there are no naive
  datetimes anywhere.**
- `pseudonym` columns are `uuid`. They are the identifier that **survives
  account deletion** and are what every analytics query groups by (§9).
- Enum values are stored as `text` with a `CHECK (col IN (...))`, not as
  Postgres `ENUM` types. Reason: adding a value to a Postgres enum inside a
  transaction with other DDL is awkward and `ALTER TYPE` cannot be rolled back
  cleanly; a CHECK constraint is a one-line migration either way.
  `# ponytail: text + CHECK over native enums. Ceiling: no type safety across
  tables. Upgrade path: none wanted.`
- **Slice** column says which slice in `TASKS.md` creates the table. Tables
  marked S5+ are specified now and built later — that is deliberate, so the
  metric queries in §9 are known to be satisfiable before anyone writes code.
- Every table that holds personal data cites its row in `COMPLIANCE.md` §7.
  **A new personal-data field needs a §7 row before it ships** — that section's
  own standing rule. Four new fields are proposed in §11 and routed to
  privacy-compliance.

### ~~`age_check_pending`~~ — **REMOVED by amendment A-5 (CG-5). No DOB is persisted anywhere, in any state.**

v1.0 of this document specified an `age_check_pending` table holding a declared
date of birth for up to seven days pending staff review. **PRD CG-5 was right
to question it, and the ruling goes further than the PRD proposed: the table is
deleted from the schema.**

**The rule, with no exception: a declared date of birth exists only inside the
HTTP request that declares it. It is never written to any table, any log, any
cache or any backup.**

How the age gate works without it:

1. The signup form carries a real `<input type="date">`
   (`COMPLIANCE.md` §1.4 item 1: *"a date field, not a checkbox"*).
2. The **server** computes the age and compares. A checkbox is not evidence; a
   server-side rejection is.
3. **Under 18** ⇒ `403 NOT_ADULT_VERIFIED`, an `age_verification_events` row
   with `outcome='REJECTED_UNDER_18'`, **and the `users` row is deleted in the
   same transaction.** The DOB is discarded with the request.
4. **18 or over** ⇒ `users.adult_verified = true` and an
   `age_verification_events` row with
   `method='SELF_DECLARED_DOB_SERVER_CHECKED'`, **in the same request.** The
   DOB is discarded with the request. No pending row, no review window, no
   ambiguous week for the student.

**What happened to the "logged human review" COMPLIANCE.md §1.4 item 3 asks
for.** It stays, and it reviews **the account, not the date.** The reviewer
sees the adult-status outcome, the verifying email domain, the campus match and
the account's behaviour, and records `method='STAFF_REVIEW'` with their id and
timestamp. This is a deliberate reading of §1.4, so it is stated rather than
assumed: **with no identity document collected, a reviewer cannot verify a
declared date against anything, so storing the date buys the review no
information and costs a week of DOB retention.** The audit trail §1.4 wants is
the reviewer, the outcome and the timestamp — all three survive, in
`age_verification_events`, for the account's life plus one year.

**Consequences of the removal, all of them simplifications:**
- The ADR-003 §2.3 deny-list exception for `date_of_birth` / `dob` / `%birth%`
  **is withdrawn. The pattern is now unconditional**, which is a stronger
  constraint and one fewer thing for a reviewer to reason about.
- `COMPLIANCE.md` §7 row 7's "DOB purged after verification" is satisfied
  trivially rather than by a job that has to run.
- One table, one retention rule and one expiry path are deleted from slice S1.
- A student who mistypes their date and is rejected may simply sign up again;
  the magic-link rate limits bound the attempts. **This is unchanged from v1.0
  and it is not a weakening: `COMPLIANCE.md` §1.4 already states that a
  self-declaration will not keep a determined 17-year-old out, and the controls
  for that are the logged review and the standing
  suspend-erase-log instruction, not the storage of the date.**

**Append-only, never updated in place** (§8.1). A withdrawal inserts a new row
with `withdrawn_at` set. Current state is the latest row per
`(user_id, purpose_key)`. **No pre-ticked boxes** (E-Com r.4(9), in force now)
and **withdrawal is a settings toggle, not an email** (DPDP r.3(c)(i)) — both
are UI contracts in `API-CONTRACT.md` §5.2.
> **RULING · append-only and a finite retention period are both kept, and the
> rule becomes a property of the ROW rather than of the caller · cto,
> 2026-10-22** *(backend S1 escalation 1; applies to `consents` AND
> `age_verification_events`)*
>
> **Backend found a real contradiction between two of my own requirements.** I
> asked for these tables to be append-only, and I asked for retention of
> "account life + 1 year". A trigger that refuses every DELETE makes the
> retention rule unimplementable — **so the contract required both a thing and
> its opposite.** Backend did not code around it: it put both tables in the
> purge skip list, tested the skip, and escalated. **That is the right handling
> of a contract defect and the alternative — quietly picking one — is how the
> inventory would have become false.**
>
> **Three options were on the table. I am taking none of them as offered.**
> - *Retain indefinitely* fails: `COMPLIANCE.md` §8.3 is explicit that
>   **"retaining beyond the purpose without a legal basis is its own breach"**,
>   and r.8(3)'s one year is a **minimum**, not a licence. Indefinite retention
>   of consent and age records would need a positive legal basis nobody has
>   asserted.
> - *Move the rows to a non-append-only table* fails worse: these are the two
>   tables whose entire value is being **evidence**. Removing tamper
>   protection from the evidence to satisfy a retention job is the wrong way
>   round.
> - *Let the trigger permit a purge actor* is the right direction and the wrong
>   mechanism. An actor- or GUC-gated escape would put the key to deleting
>   consent evidence in a **production code path used routinely** — and I have
>   already ruled (ADR-001 §2.2) that **no mechanism can make production refuse
>   a GUC.** That was tolerable for a test-harness escape on TRUNCATE; it is
>   not tolerable for a job that runs every night against consent records.
>
> **The ruling: append-only is specified as a predicate on the row, with no
> privileged caller at all.**
>
> | Operation | Permitted? |
> |---|---|
> | `UPDATE` | **Only** to set `purge_due_at` from NULL to non-NULL. Every other column immutable |
> | `DELETE` | **Only** when `purge_due_at IS NOT NULL AND purge_due_at <= now()` |
> | `TRUNCATE` | Never |
>
> `purge_due_at` is **NULL for the life of the account** — so a live consent or
> age record is undeletable by anyone, including the expiry job — and is set to
> `tombstoned_at + 1 year` by `erase_account` (§10.1), which is why the UPDATE
> limb exists at all.
>
> **Why this is better than any actor-based rule: there is nothing to
> impersonate.** The job has no privilege the attacker lacks; the row's own
> retention state is the whole authorisation. An early DELETE fails for the
> expiry job exactly as it fails for a compromised session.
>
> **And it is less new machinery than it sounds — the pattern is already in the
> repository and already executed on PostgreSQL:** migration 0002's
> `gate_authorisations` trigger permits UPDATE of `revoked_at`/`revoked_reason`
> and nothing else. This is the same trigger shape with a different column list
> and a time predicate. **Both engine halves are required, and the trigger
> inventory test (see below) must cover them.**
>
> **To privacy-compliance, as a note and not a request:** §7 rows 7 and 18 say
> "not user-deletable", which stays exactly true — **the user cannot delete
> these rows; the expiry job can, once the stated period has elapsed.** The
> inventory's retention column already says "+1 year", so the mechanism now
> matches the inventory rather than contradicting it. Confirm if you disagree.

Retention: account life **+ 1 year**. Not user-deletable — it is the record
that the deletion itself was lawful.

### `idempotency_keys` — S0

> **`user_id` carries NO foreign key — backend's decision, CONFIRMED
> 2026-10-22, and confirming it exposes a wrong premise of mine.** Backend's
> reasoning is right: the break an FK would guard **cannot arise**, because
> `erase_account` **tombstones rather than deletes** the `users` row (§10.1),
> so a `user_id` here always points at a surviving row — and with 24-hour
> retention no key outlives even the one-year purge.
>
> **The honest part: I rejected the `user_id = 0` sentinel (A-13) partly
> *because* "it breaks the foreign key to `users` that S1 adds". S1 added no
> such key, so that premise was false.** The conclusion stands on its other
> leg — a partial unique index needs no extra column holding a value derived
> from another — and that is the leg it should have rested on. **Recorded
> rather than quietly left, because a conclusion propped up by a wrong premise
> is one someone re-opens the moment they find the premise.**

**Two unique indexes, not one — amendment A-13, corrected.**
```sql
UNIQUE (user_id, key)                                  -- authenticated callers
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX idempotency_anon ON idempotency_keys (key)
  WHERE user_id IS NULL;                               -- anonymous callers
```
**`key` format (amendment A-23): a UUID is REQUIRED when `user_id IS NULL` and
unconstrained otherwise.** The asymmetry rests on the invariant in
`API-CONTRACT.md` §4.1 — *`user_id` partitions the key namespace, one principal
per partition* — and **extends to the authenticated namespace the moment any
endpoint writes a key on another principal's behalf**, which S5 support and S6
moderation are the likely first occasions for. An anonymous replay returns the
stored status with a **NULL body**, so a guessed key cannot leak another
caller's response.
v1.0 specified only the first, so **a NULL `user_id` made the constraint not
fire and a replay claimed a second row instead of replaying the first**
(QA-S0 B3). My first correction proposed a `user_id = 0` sentinel; **that was
wrong — it breaks the FK to `users` that S1 adds.** The partial index gives the
same property with no extra column. Anonymous keys share one global namespace,
which is sound for a client UUIDv4. See `API-CONTRACT.md` §4.1.

**States (11):** `OFFERED`, `DECLINED`*, `ACCEPTED`, `SCHEDULED`,

**Guards, in full:**

| Transition | Guard |
|---|---|
| `→ OFFERED` | listing `status='LIVE'`; buyer ≠ seller; buyer `adult_verified AND campus_verified AND status='ACTIVE'`; campus discovery gate open **or** both parties seed; no existing non-terminal transfer by this buyer on this listing; neither party has blocked the other |
| `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` | actor is seller; **the partial unique index is the guard** — any other active transfer on the listing makes this fail; listing still `LIVE`; sets `listing.status='RESERVED'` and `reservation_expires_at = now() + 48h` in the same transaction |
| `OFFERED → DECLINED` | actor is seller |
| `OFFERED / ACCEPTED / SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` | actor is buyer, seller or staff; records `cancelled_by` + `cancel_reason_code`; relists the listing if it was RESERVED |
| `ACCEPTED → SCHEDULED` | both parties have agreed a `handover_point_id` (active, same campus) and a window; `handover_window_start > now()`; window ≤ 14 days out |
| **`SCHEDULED → ACCEPTED`** *(amendment A-1, CG-1)* | **actor is buyer or seller** (not SYSTEM, not staff); `now() < handover_window_end`; **fewer than 2 prior reschedules on this transfer**, counted as `transfer_events` rows with `reason_code='RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED'`; transfer is not `DISPUTED`. **Sets `reservation_expires_at = now() + 48h`** and leaves `handover_point_id` and the window in place until `propose_handover` overwrites them, so the UI can show what the old slot was. **`accepted_at` is NOT rewritten** — see the note below |
| `ACCEPTED → EXPIRED` | SYSTEM only, `now() > reservation_expires_at`; relists |
| `SCHEDULED → AWAITING_CONFIRMATION` | SYSTEM at `handover_window_end`, **or** either party confirms early |
| `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW` | a party reports no-show ≥15 min after `handover_window_start` (`TEST-1` §3.5 definition); relists; **both parties notified, no automatic penalty** |
| `AWAITING_CONFIRMATION → COMPLETED` | both `seller_confirmed_at` and `buyer_confirmed_at` non-NULL. **One side alone is never a completion** (`TEST-1` §3.5) |
| `AWAITING_CONFIRMATION → EXPIRED` | SYSTEM, 72h after entering the state with 3 follow-up attempts made; sets `leakage_class` per §4.1 of TEST-1 |
| `* → DISPUTED` | actor is buyer or seller, within 7 days of `handover_window_end` or `completed_at`; creates a `disputes` row |
| `DISPUTED → COMPLETED / CANCELLED / CLOSED_UNRESOLVED` | staff only, with a `disputes.resolution` value |

**Idempotency and concurrency:** every transition endpoint takes
`Idempotency-Key` **and** `expected_version`. The service does
`UPDATE transfers SET state=…, version=version+1 WHERE id=? AND version=?`;
zero rows affected ⇒ `409 VERSION_CONFLICT`. A replayed request with the same
idempotency key returns the stored response without re-running the transition.
Every transition writes a `transfer_events` row **in the same transaction** as
the state change — so the event log can never disagree with the state.

**Timers, all run by the `jobs` runner once a minute:**
`reservation.expire`, `handover.window_closed`, `followup.ask` (+0h, +24h,
+48h after entering AWAITING_CONFIRMATION), `followup.classify` (+72h),
`dispute.sla_warn`.

### 6.3 The reschedule, and the three things it would have broken silently

Added by **amendment A-1** on PRD **CG-1**. The PRD's case is accepted in full
and its reasoning is the right reasoning: the alternative to a reschedule
transition is not "no reschedule", it is **cancel-and-re-offer**, which
destroys a live match, relists a book that is not actually available, counts
against a pre-registered cancellation bar, and pushes the coordination into
chat — where it becomes `COORD` support minutes. **We would have been
measuring our own omission with the instrument D-009's reversal trigger
reads.**

The PRD said "no schema change" and that is correct — no column is added. But
three consequences were not in the PRD and each is a silent corruption if
missed. They are normative:

1. **`reservation_expires_at` must be reset to `now() + 48h` on the
   reschedule.** Returning to `ACCEPTED` re-arms `reservation.expire`, and by
   the time a pair have scheduled and then rescheduled, the original 48-hour
   window has almost certainly passed — so without the reset the transfer would
   be expired by the job runner within the minute. **This is also the second
   reason the cap of 2 matters**: without a cap, repeated rescheduling would
   renew the reservation indefinitely and hold a book off the market, which is
   the hostage case the PRD identified by a different route.
2. **`accepted_at` is write-once.** It is set on the *first* entry to
   `ACCEPTED` and is never rewritten. Time to match is
   `accepted_at − listings.published_at`; rewriting it on a reschedule would
   make every rescheduled transfer look like it matched days later than it did.
3. **Every "ever reached state X" denominator must count DISTINCT transfers,
   not events.** A reschedule puts a second `ACCEPTED` event and a second
   `SCHEDULED` event on the same transfer. §9.1 and §9.3 are amended
   accordingly.

**A reschedule is never a cancellation in any query.** This needs no query
change — cancellation rate counts `to_state='CANCELLED'`, which a reschedule
never writes — but it is asserted by a test (`THREAT-MODEL.md` §6) because it
is the kind of thing a later analytics edit breaks.

**Job interaction.** A `handover.window_closed` job may already be enqueued for
a transfer that has since been rescheduled. No special handling is needed: §4.4
already requires every job handler to check current state and no-op, and
`handover.window_closed` acts only on `SCHEDULED`. Stated so nobody adds a
cancel-the-job mechanism that is not required.

### 6.4 Events that are not transitions — the relay

Added by **amendment A-6**. This gap was **not** in the PRD's list of five;
`JOURNEYS.md` J5c step 2 and `PRD.md` AC-M4.1 both specify a relay and the PRD
states it *"needs no contract amendment"*. **That is the one place the PRD is
wrong, and it is my gap to close, not product's:** the relay has no L1
function, no endpoint and no notification kind in v1.0 of the contract, so as
frozen it cannot happen.

It matters more than its size. `THREAT-MODEL.md` §5.8 and accepted risk A1
record that at one staff member the two-person rule on
`contact_disclosures` **blocks** rather than reviews. Product's answer is
correct and better than weakening the rule: **stop the common case from
arriving at it.** The relay is that change, so it is the mitigation for my own
accepted risk and it ships with the disclosure flow, not after it.

- **Not a state transition.** The transfer stays in `ACCEPTED` or `SCHEDULED`.
- It writes a `transfer_events` row with `reason_code='RELAY_SENT'` and **no
  state change** (`from_state = to_state`), which is the first such event in
  the system — so `transfer_events` is hereby defined to carry
  non-transition events where `from_state = to_state`, and every state metric
  in §9 already filters on `to_state` transitions it names, so none is
  affected.
- It reopens the listing thread and sends a `COUNTERPARTY_NUDGE` notification.
- **No `contact_disclosures` row, no staff queue, no two-person rule.** QA
  asserts the relay path writes no disclosure row (`THREAT-MODEL.md` §6).
- **It is what unlocks the disclosure request:** `request_contact_disclosure`
  now guards on **two `RELAY_SENT` events at least 72 hours apart with no
  intervening message from the counterparty** (AC-M4.2). Without the relay
  there was nothing to count, which is why the guard was absent in v1.0.
- Rate limit: 1 relay per transfer per 24 hours, max 2 per transfer.

### `audit_log` — S0 · append-only, tamper-evident
`(id, actor_type CHECK IN ('USER','STAFF','SYSTEM'), actor_id NULL,

> **`actor_id` carries NO foreign key — CONFIRMED 2026-10-22, and the reason
> generalises into a standing hazard that is bigger than this column.**
>
> Backend's reason: adding the constraint makes Django **rebuild the table on
> SQLite** (create-new, copy, drop-old, rename — SQLite's `ALTER TABLE`
> emulation), and **a rebuild silently drops every trigger attached to the old
> table.** So the migration that adds an FK to `audit_log` would remove the
> append-only guard — *the defect of the slice, introduced by a constraint
> intended to improve integrity.* Confirmed, and the FK buys little anyway:
> `actor_id` is NULLed by `erase_account` and the durable identifier is
> `actor_pseudonym`.
>
> **The general hazard, which is the part worth keeping: on SQLite, ANY
> migration that alters a triggered table silently drops its triggers.** That
> is now four tables and will be five — `audit_log`, `gate_authorisations`,
> `campuses`, `consents`/`age_verification_events` once the expired-only-DELETE
> triggers land, and `listings` from S2. **And the divergence is the dangerous
> shape: PostgreSQL's `ALTER TABLE` does not rebuild, so the guard survives
> there and vanishes on SQLite — one engine protected, one not, from one

>
> **The control needs BOTH limbs — corrected 2026-10-22, because the
> structural half I specified here was found passing while the property was
> broken.** `tests/contract/test_append_only_is_enforced.py`:
> **(a) behavioural, per table per engine — UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE are
> each refused**, which is the guarantee and fails whatever the cause; and
> **(b) structural — the exact expected trigger set** from `pg_trigger` /
> `sqlite_master`, which is the diagnostic and fails *by name* so the message
> says what went missing, rather than surfacing as a surprise red where **the
> tempting fix is to skip the test** (rule 13's shape).
>
> **v1.0 of this paragraph specified only (b), on the reasoning that "a
> behavioural test only catches it if someone wrote one for that table on that
> engine". That reasoning was right about coverage and wrong about
> sufficiency:** QA found (b) green on PostgreSQL while `audit_log` was freely
> updatable, because a surviving **sibling** trigger satisfied the existence
> check. **An existence check diagnoses; only a behavioural check
> guarantees** — `THREAT-MODEL.md` §6.3.
>
> **Standing rule for backend: a migration that alters a triggered table
> re-creates that table's triggers in the same migration.** `ENGINE-DIVERGENCE`
> is the right home for the engine detail; the rule belongs wherever the

`row_hash = sha256(prev_hash || canonical_json(row minus hashes))` — a hash
chain, ~15 lines. Covers consent grants/withdrawals, age decisions, moderation
actions, takedowns and restorations, contact disclosures, staff data access,
deletion executions (`COMPLIANCE.md` §8.5).
`# ponytail: single-writer hash chain, verified by a management command.
Ceiling: assumes one appender and does not prove the chain head was not
rewritten. Upgrade path: write the daily chain head to a second place —
printing it in the daily digest email is enough and costs nothing.`

#### DECISION · the chain stays GLOBAL · cto, 2026-10-21 (ENGINE-DIVERGENCE §9.15)

The first PostgreSQL run established that **`prev_hash UNIQUE` serialises every
audit append by construction.** Backend correctly fixed the retry budget and
left the design to me. Three options were on the table: stay global with a
retry budget, split into **per-scope chains**, or move to **periodic checkpoint
anchoring**.

**Ruling: the chain stays global. Do not build per-scope chains or checkpoint
anchoring for the pilot.** Three reasons, and the second is the one that makes
this more than a cost decision:

1. **The measured headroom is not a close call.** 40 concurrent appenders all
   land and the chain verifies. The pilot — one campus, a few hundred accounts
   — writes single-digit audit rows per minute at peak. That is orders of
   magnitude of margin, not a tight fit.
2. **Per-scope chains would weaken the exact property the chain exists for.**
   A single global chain means the whole audit log is **one** tamper-evident
   sequence: no row anywhere can be removed without breaking it. **Split it per
   campus and an insider can delete one campus's entire audit history while
   every other chain still verifies clean** — a gap that is invisible because
   there is no longer anything linking the scopes. That is a direct regression
   against §5.8 and accepted risk A1, which is the whole reason the chain is
   here. **Per-scope is not a free performance win; it is a trade against the
   threat model, and at pilot write rates we would be paying it for nothing.**
3. **Checkpoint anchoring is the right upgrade when one is needed, and it does
   not have that weakness** — it keeps a single sequence and removes the
   per-row serialisation point by hashing batches and chaining the checkpoints.
   Its cost is **coarser localisation**: you learn that something in a batch
   changed, and need the batch to say what. Acceptable at scale, and it buys
   nothing now.

**The engineering standard also points here:** no scaffolding for a future that
may not arrive, on a pilot that may be killed by its own data (R-21, D-009).

**Re-measure trigger — an observable, not a feeling.** Any one of:
- **p99 attempts-to-win exceeds 6** (half the budget of 12) over a rolling
  day — a *leading* indicator, with headroom before anything fails;
- **any `AuditAppendContention` at all** in a day — the budget was exhausted,
  which is already a failed mutation;
- **the first month of real traffic on a real table size**, unconditionally.

**And the honest limit of the measurement the budget rests on: 40 appenders on
a near-empty table establishes that the mechanism survives contention. It does
not establish a throughput ceiling at scale.** The head read is trivial on an
empty table; with a year of retained rows the insert contends on a hot
rightmost index page, and the nightly retention purge runs against the same
table. **The budget is sized from data, and the data is about the mechanism,
not about a campus under load.**

#### The operational signal — because splitting the error class is only half the fix

Backend split budget-exhaustion out of `AuditChainBroken` into
`AuditAppendContention`, deliberately **not** a subclass, so a load spike no
longer fires a tamper alert. That is right: the old behaviour reported **write
contention as tampering**, which would have sent an operator hunting a breach
during a traffic peak.

**But splitting an error class without adding a watcher converts a false alarm
into a silent failure.** So both branches are watched, and the contention
branch is watched by a leading indicator rather than a lagging one:

| Signal | Where | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Attempts-to-win per append, p50 and p99** | counter → daily digest (`INFRA.md` §5) | informational; **p99 > 6 is the re-measure trigger above** |
| **`AuditAppendContention` count** | alert | **pages on-call if > 0 in an hour.** The budget was exhausted and a mutation failed |
| **`AuditChainBroken`** | alert | **founder escalation.** It asserts tampering and must keep meaning that |

**Recording the attempts, not only the exhaustions, is the part that matters.**
Without it we learn about contention only when it has already broken something
— which is the same silent-until-it-breaks shape the split was meant to fix.

### `personal_data_access_log` — S1
`(id, staff_id FK, subject_pseudonym uuid, fields_accessed text[], purpose,
request_id, occurred_at)` · §7 row 16.
DPDP r.6(1)(c) requires *"visibility on the accessing of such personal
data"*. Every admin view of a student's profile or chat writes a row. **The
staff member sees that this is logged**, which is most of the control
(`THREAT-MODEL.md` §5.8).

### `jobs` — S0
`(id, kind, payload jsonb, run_after timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
attempts smallint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, max_attempts smallint NOT NULL DEFAULT 5,
locked_at, locked_by, completed_at, failed_at, last_error, created_at)`
Index `(run_after) WHERE completed_at IS NULL AND failed_at IS NULL`.
`SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (ADR-001 §2.4). At-least-once, so every
handler is idempotent.

### `purge_log` — S1 · proof the expiry job ran
`(id, table_name, object_pk, purge_due_at, purged_at, job_run_id, mode CHECK IN
('PURGE','PSEUDONYMISE'))`
`COMPLIANCE.md` §8.3: *"Retaining beyond the purpose without a legal basis is
its own breach. Build the expiry job at the same time as the retention."*
This table is how we prove it to G3/G4.

### 9.2 Repeat transfer @90d — **the decisive one (R-24, O-10)**

Why the schema makes it observable: `transfers` carries `buyer_pseudonym` and
`seller_pseudonym`, which are **assigned at account creation and retained on
the tombstone for one year after deletion**. So the cohort survives erasure, and
a 90-day window is computable even if every participant deletes their account
on day 30.

```sql
-- "First-time recipients completing another transfer <=90d"
-- (METRICS.md denominator: first-time recipients cohort)
WITH completed AS (
  SELECT id, buyer_pseudonym AS pseudonym, completed_at
  FROM transfers
  WHERE state = 'COMPLETED' AND NOT buyer_is_seed
),
first_receipt AS (
  SELECT pseudonym, MIN(completed_at) AS first_at
  FROM completed GROUP BY pseudonym
),
cohort AS (                      -- only people whose 90-day window has elapsed
  SELECT * FROM first_receipt
  WHERE first_at <= now() - interval '90 days'
),
repeaters AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT c.pseudonym
  FROM cohort c
  JOIN transfers t
    ON t.state = 'COMPLETED'
   AND (t.buyer_pseudonym = c.pseudonym OR t.seller_pseudonym = c.pseudonym)
   AND t.completed_at >  c.first_at
   AND t.completed_at <= c.first_at + interval '90 days'
)
SELECT (SELECT count(*) FROM repeaters)::numeric
         / NULLIF((SELECT count(*) FROM cohort), 0)  AS repeat_transfer_90d,
       (SELECT count(*) FROM cohort)                  AS cohort_n,
       (SELECT count(*) FROM repeaters)               AS repeaters_n;
```

A second variant counts a repeat only as a **buyer** (strict reading of
"recipients"); both are in `apps/analytics/sql/` and the weekly export prints
both with their n.

**Two honest limits, which the schema cannot fix:**
1. A user who deletes and re-registers gets a new pseudonym and **is
   undercounted**. We could link via `user_tombstones.email_hash`, but that
   hash exists for ban evasion and dispute linkage only (§7 row 3) and using it
   for analytics is purpose creep. **We accept the undercount.**
2. The metric needs ≥90 days of operation. **The schema makes it observable;
   only the pilot's duration decides whether it is observed.** R-24 stays open
   and this query is the thing that closes it.

### 9.3 Match rate — **the decisive one for liquidity (R-01)**

`METRICS.md`: *searches/requests leading to an accepted offer*, denominator
*distinct searching users/week*.

```sql
WITH searchers AS (                      -- denominator
  SELECT date_trunc('week', occurred_at) AS week,
         count(DISTINCT actor_pseudonym)  AS searching_users
  FROM search_events
  WHERE actor_pseudonym IS NOT NULL
  GROUP BY 1
),
first_accept AS (     -- amendment A-1: a reschedule writes a SECOND 'ACCEPTED'
  SELECT transfer_id, MIN(occurred_at) AS first_accepted_at
  FROM transfer_events WHERE to_state = 'ACCEPTED' GROUP BY transfer_id
),
matched AS (                             -- numerator: FIRST reached ACCEPTED that week
  SELECT date_trunc('week', fa.first_accepted_at) AS week,
         count(DISTINCT t.buyer_pseudonym)         AS matched_users
  FROM first_accept fa
  JOIN transfers t ON t.id = fa.transfer_id
  WHERE NOT t.buyer_is_seed
  GROUP BY 1
)
SELECT s.week,
       s.searching_users,
       COALESCE(m.matched_users, 0)                                   AS matched_users,
       COALESCE(m.matched_users, 0)::numeric / NULLIF(s.searching_users, 0) AS match_rate
FROM searchers s LEFT JOIN matched m USING (week)
ORDER BY s.week;

-- Request-side limb: a stated want that became a completed transfer.
-- This is the one that exposes illiquidity, because an unfulfilled want is a row.
-- Amendment A-3 (CG-3): fulfilled_by_transfer_id is now WRITTEN by
-- confirm_handover. Before that amendment this query returned zero forever.
SELECT date_trunc('week', r.created_at) AS week,
       count(*)                                               AS requests,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.book_id IS NULL)              AS unmatchable_free_text,
       count(r.fulfilled_by_transfer_id)                      AS fulfilled,
       count(r.fulfilled_by_transfer_id)::numeric / NULLIF(count(*), 0) AS request_fill_rate
FROM book_requests r
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
```

> **Amendment A-3 (PRD CG-3), accepted.** The column existed, this query read
> it, and **no L1 function wrote it** — so one of the three decisive metrics
> would have reported zero permanently while every row needed to compute it sat
> in the database. That is the worst kind of measurement bug: it looks like a
> finding ("nobody's wants get filled") rather than like a defect.
>
> **`confirm_handover`, in the same transaction that reaches `COMPLETED`**,
> closes any `OPEN` `book_requests` where
> `requester_pseudonym = transfer.buyer_pseudonym AND book_id = transfer.book_id
> AND campus_id = transfer.campus_id`, setting `status='FULFILLED'`,
> `fulfilled_by_transfer_id` and `closed_at`. One query, no schema change, no
> new endpoint.
>
> Two boundaries: it **never overwrites** an already-`FULFILLED` row, and it
> **cannot touch a `free_text_title` request** (`book_id IS NULL`), which has
> no book to match on. The `unmatchable_free_text` column above makes that
> exclusion visible in the metric rather than silently depressing the fill
> rate — because a free-text want is a catalogue gap, not a liquidity failure,
> and conflating the two would misread R-01.

**R-27 note carried forward:** Test 1 could not measure search success because
it had no search surface. The app can, and this is the first real measurement
of R-01. Human match rate from the concierge design was an upper bound only;
these two numbers are not comparable to it and the weekly export says so.

### 9.4 Support time per transfer — **the decisive one (R-13, R-25, D-009's reversal trigger)**

Both of `TEST-1` §3.4's numbers, with the same definitions, so the figures are
comparable to the concierge design's thresholds.

```sql
-- (1) THE PASS NUMBER: median support minutes per COMPLETED handover.
--     Threshold <= 4.00 min. D-009 reversal trigger fires above it at n >= 30.
WITH per_transfer AS (
  SELECT t.id, SUM(w.seconds) / 60.0 AS minutes
  FROM transfers t
  JOIN support_tickets  s ON s.transfer_id = t.id
  JOIN support_work_log w ON w.ticket_id   = s.id
  WHERE t.state = 'COMPLETED' AND NOT t.buyer_is_seed
    AND t.completed_at >= :from AND t.completed_at < :to
  GROUP BY t.id
)
SELECT percentile_cont(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY minutes) AS median_minutes,
       count(*)                                            AS n_completed_with_work,
       percentile_cont(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY minutes) * 2.84 AS median_rupees
FROM per_transfer;

-- (2) THE MODEL NUMBER: fully-loaded mean. Every minute logged in the window
--     -- failed matches, no-shows, disputes, rejected listings, DAILY_UNALLOCATED --
--     divided by completed handovers. Nothing excluded for being a failure.
WITH all_minutes AS (
  SELECT SUM(seconds) / 60.0 AS m
  FROM support_work_log
  WHERE started_at >= :from AND started_at < :to
),
all_minutes_no_chase AS (
  SELECT SUM(seconds) / 60.0 AS m
  FROM support_work_log
  WHERE started_at >= :from AND started_at < :to AND bucket <> 'CHASE'
),
completions AS (
  SELECT count(*) AS n FROM transfers
  WHERE state = 'COMPLETED' AND NOT buyer_is_seed
    AND completed_at >= :from AND completed_at < :to
)
SELECT (SELECT m FROM all_minutes)          / NULLIF((SELECT n FROM completions),0)
         AS fully_loaded_mean_minutes,      -- PRE-REGISTERED PASS NUMBER
       (SELECT m FROM all_minutes_no_chase) / NULLIF((SELECT n FROM completions),0)
         AS mean_minutes_chase_removed,     -- "instrument cost removed, NOT the pass number"
       (SELECT m FROM all_minutes)          / NULLIF((SELECT n FROM completions),0) * 2.84
         AS fully_loaded_rupees;

-- (3) Per-bucket breakdown, so a bad number can be acted on rather than argued about.
SELECT bucket, SUM(seconds)/60.0 AS minutes, count(*) AS entries
FROM support_work_log
WHERE started_at >= :from AND started_at < :to
GROUP BY bucket ORDER BY 2 DESC;
```

Rs 2.84 per minute from `evidence/RED-TEAM/G1-MEMO.md` O-4 (Rs 30,000/month
fully loaded). The export prints the three reference lines from `TEST-1` §3.4 —
**4.00 min = Rs 11.36 (pass, "not catastrophic"), 2.11 min = Rs 6.00 (unit
turns positive), 1.06 min = Rs 3.00 (support alone flips the sign)** — and the
sentence *"passing the 4-minute bar means 'not catastrophic', not 'viable'"*,
because R-25 is a documentary mitigation and this is where the document is.

### 9.5 Leakage band (O-7 / O-10 indicator)

```sql
WITH c AS (
  SELECT leakage_class, count(*) n FROM transfers
  WHERE leakage_class IS NOT NULL AND NOT buyer_is_seed GROUP BY 1
),
k AS (SELECT COALESCE(SUM(n),0) v FROM c WHERE leakage_class IN
        ('IN_APP_CONFIRMED','ONE_SIDED_CONFIRMED','SILENT_CONFIRMED')),
u AS (SELECT COALESCE(SUM(n),0) v FROM c WHERE leakage_class IN
        ('SILENT_PARTIAL','UNKNOWN_NO_REPLY','UNKNOWN_TRUNCATED')),
s AS (SELECT COALESCE(SUM(n),0) v FROM c WHERE leakage_class='SILENT_CONFIRMED')
SELECT (SELECT v FROM s)::numeric / NULLIF((SELECT v FROM k),0)              AS lower_bound,
       ((SELECT v FROM s) + (SELECT v FROM u))::numeric
         / NULLIF((SELECT v FROM k) + (SELECT v FROM u),0)                   AS upper_bound,
       (SELECT v FROM k) AS k_known, (SELECT v FROM u) AS u_unknown;
```
**Reported as a band, never as a point. If the band is wider than 20
percentage points the export states that this was not measured** — there is no
midpoint (`TEST-1` §4.1).

## 10. Retention and deletion, per field

Reconciled row-by-row with `COMPLIANCE.md` §7. **Two floors override any
shorter period: DPDP r.8(3) (personal data, traffic data, processing logs — 1
year) and r.6(1)(e) (security logs — 1 year). CERT-In's 180 days is subsumed.**

### 10.1 What "delete my account" does

`COMPLIANCE.md` §7: *"'Delete my account' never means `DELETE FROM users`. It
means: purge rows 1–9 and 15, pseudonymise 10–13, and leave 14 and 16–18
alone."* Implemented as one transactional service function,
`accounts.services.erase_account(user)`:

| Step | Action | §7 rows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Insert `user_tombstones` (pseudonym, campus, city, **salted** `email_hash`, `purge_due_at = now() + 1 year`) | — |
| 2 | `users`: NULL `email`, `display_name`, `password`; `status='TOMBSTONED'`; `tombstoned_at=now()` | 1, 3, 4 |
| 3 | Delete `email_login_tokens`, `push_subscriptions`, `sessions`, `notification_preferences` | 4 |
| 4 | Delete `campus_verifications` rows; `users.campus_verified` stays as a boolean | 5, 6 |
| 5 | `listing_photos`: schedule object deletion at `listing.closed_at + 1 year` (**not now** — the listing is part of the transaction record) | 9 |
| 6 | `listings`, `book_requests`, `transfers`, `threads`, `messages`, `reports`, `disputes`, **`support_tickets`, `support_messages`** (A-2): NULL the `*_id` FK, **keep the `*_pseudonym`**, set `purge_due_at` | 10–13, +support |
| 7 | **Untouched:** `repeat_infringer_records`, `audit_log`, `personal_data_access_log`, `age_verification_events`, `consents`, `security logs` | 14, 16, 17, 18, 7 |
| 8 | `data_subject_requests` row completed; `audit_log` row written | — |

**Pseudonymisation is one-way**: the retained record carries only the
pseudonym, and the pseudonym→identity mapping is destroyed in step 2. The
`user_tombstones.email_hash` is salted with a **server-side secret not stored
in the database**, so a database-only compromise cannot re-identify by
rainbow-table.

#### 10.1a · Erasure has TWO independent layers, and the second one is the guarantee

**Specified 2026-10-22 on QA's erasure pass (S-2). This layer already exists in
the code and existed in no document — it was doing the real work of the
guarantee by accident of implementation, which is precisely the thing a later
refactor deletes without noticing.**

| | Layer | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Session destruction at erasure** — step 3 above | Scans and deletes the erased user's session rows. **Best-effort** |
| **2** | **Tombstone refusal at request time** | **Every authenticated request resolves the actor and refuses if `users.status = 'TOMBSTONED'`, before any other precondition, regardless of whether the session is valid.** The guarantee |

**Layer 1 is best-effort, and QA measured three ways it can match nothing:**
a signing-key rotation without fallback, a changed session serialiser, and a
**cookie-backed session store, where there are no rows to scan at all.** In two
of those **a session row survives carrying the erased student's user id,
decodable again if the key or the serialiser comes back.**

**And yet no live session results — because layer 2 refuses the user, not the
session.** QA proved it the right way round, by planting a *brand-new* session
after erasure and finding it dead on arrival. **That is the difference between
invalidating credentials and invalidating the principal**, and only the second
survives a session store we did not anticipate.

This is `CLAUDE.md` rule 14 in its intended form: **the exemption-free layer is
the one that does not depend on enumerating how sessions are stored.** Layer 1
stays — it frees storage and shortens the window — but **no document may cite
layer 1 as the reason erasure holds.**

> **WARNING, and it is the reason this is specified rather than merely
> recorded: layer 2 refuses `TOMBSTONED` ONLY, and must not be "hardened" to
> refuse `SUSPENDED`.** QA noted the asymmetry as a possible gap. **It is not a
> gap — it is the behaviour `API-CONTRACT.md` §2.5 Stack S now requires**: a
> suspended student must still reach export, erasure, consent withdrawal and
> their own support ticket. **Extending layer 2 to suspension would silently
> re-create P-1**, and would do it in the one place no endpoint-level review
> would look. A tombstoned account is gone; a suspended account is a person we
> have sanctioned who keeps their rights.

**The test is QA's and it is already written.** The specification's job is to
make the layer survive a refactor, and the one sentence that does that is:
**an authenticated request from a tombstoned principal is refused even when the
session is wholly valid.**

### 10.2 The expiry job — built in the same slice as the retention

`retention.purge` runs nightly, walks every table with a `purge_due_at`, and
for each due row either hard-deletes or nulls content per the table's rule,
writing a `purge_log` row for each. **Explicit skip-list:**
`repeat_infringer_records` (r.5(5) — survives), and any row whose
`preserved_until` (removed content, r.3(1)(j) 180 days) or open
`safety_incidents`/`disputes`/`legal_notices` holds it.

`# ponytail: nightly table-walk delete. Ceiling: when a purge run takes longer
than the night. Upgrade path: monthly range partitions on the three
unbounded tables (ADR-002 §6 step 4), then DROP PARTITION.`

### 10.3 Acceptance test, from `COMPLIANCE.md` §8.3

`tests/e2e/test_deletion.py`, owned by **test-qa** and written independently:
delete a test account, then assert — (a) the transfer record survives; (b) it

measurement coexist**, which is the central claim of this data model.

## 13. Open contract questions — recorded so reinstating a deferred feature is not archaeology

### OQ-1 · A swap reserves two listings; the partial unique index is per listing

Raised by `JOURNEYS.md` J-DEFERRED item 4, which correctly declined to design
it now. **Recorded, not designed — that is the right call and this section
exists so the question is not rediscovered.**

**The problem.** Double-booking is prevented by
`CREATE UNIQUE INDEX one_active_transfer_per_listing ON transfers (listing_id)
WHERE state IN (...)` (§5). That guarantee is **per listing**. A direct swap
moves two books, so it reserves **two** listings, and the current index cannot
express "these two listings are held by one agreement".

**The two candidate designs, with what each would cost.** Neither is chosen.

| Design | Shape | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Two linked transfers** | Two `transfers` rows, each with its own listing, joined by a shared `swap_group_id uuid`. The existing index keeps working unchanged on both. | Every state transition must move **both** rows atomically or neither, so the state machine gains a pairing invariant: no transfer in a swap group may be `COMPLETED` while its partner is not. Dual confirmation becomes four confirmations. `disputes.transfer_id` points at one row, so a disputed swap needs resolving on the group. |
| **(b) One transfer, two listing references** | Add `counter_listing_id bigint NULL FK` to `transfers`, plus a **second** partial unique index on `(counter_listing_id)` over the same active states. | One row, one state, one confirmation pair — much simpler to reason about. But `transfers.listing_id` is `NOT NULL` and asymmetric (seller/buyer), and a swap has no buyer and no seller, so the role columns stop meaning what they mean everywhere else. Every analytics query that reads `listing_id` would need to know about the second column. |

**My current leaning, recorded as a leaning and not a decision: (b)**, because
the metrics all key off one `transfers` row per agreement (§9) and design (a)
would double-count every swap in completion rate, leakage and repeat transfer
unless every query learned about swap groups. Design (a)'s advantage —
reusing the existing index untouched — is the smaller prize.

**Two further items from J-DEFERRED that belong with it:**
- `asking_price_paise` is `NOT NULL`, so a pure-swap listing stores **`0`**,
  and **`0` must never render as "free"** — it renders as "swap only". A copy
  trap, and a `test_forbidden_copy.py` case when swap returns.
- `disputes` has one `transfer_id` and a disputed swap has two books and no
  money, so the resolution vocabulary needs a look.

**What does not need revisiting:** no money is involved, so ADR-003, P14 and
the whole fee question are untouched. Swap is the one deferred feature free of
the monetisation argument.

**Trigger to answer OQ-1:** a `DECISIONS.md` entry reversing D-007 item 3.
Nothing before that.
